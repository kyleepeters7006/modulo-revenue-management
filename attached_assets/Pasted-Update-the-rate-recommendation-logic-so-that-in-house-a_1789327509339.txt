Update the rate recommendation logic so that in-house and street rates are optimized together, using the following priority order:

1. Prioritize in-house increases first. In-house increases are the most dependable source of revenue because they apply to existing residents. Maximize reasonable in-house increases within the allowed increase range before relying on street-rate increases to achieve the revenue growth target.
2. Use variance to street to determine in-house increases. Residents who are further below the comparable street rate should generally receive larger increases, while residents already close to street should receive smaller increases. The goal is to gradually move existing residents closer to market without creating increases that could materially increase move-out risk.
3. Then optimize street rates based on market conditions. After calculating the revenue growth generated by in-house increases, determine how much of the remaining revenue target needs to come from street pricing. Street-rate recommendations should consider occupancy, vacancy, days vacant, competitor pricing, demand, historical time-to-sell, and other available demand indicators. High occupancy and strong demand should support higher street rates; weak occupancy or unfavorable competitive positioning should limit street increases.
4. Keep street rates above in-house rates whenever reasonably possible. The preferred relationship is:

Street Rate ≥ In-House Rate

However, do not artificially increase street rates just to maintain this relationship if occupancy, demand, or competitor pricing does not support it.

5. Keep in-house rates reasonably close to street rates. Avoid allowing a large gap to develop between what existing residents pay and what new residents are quoted. A large gap means we are leaving guaranteed revenue on the table and may eventually require an excessively large in-house increase. Use allowable in-house increases to gradually close this gap.

The optimization sequence should therefore be:

Revenue Growth Target → Maximize Reasonable In-House Growth → Calculate Remaining Growth Needed → Optimize Street Rates Based on Occupancy/Competition/Demand → Reconcile Street vs. In-House Rate Relationship

Do NOT simply apply the same percentage increase to both rates.

The model should explicitly favor a dollar of reasonable in-house revenue growth over a dollar of projected street-rate growth because the in-house revenue is much more certain.

For every recommendation, show the reasoning in plain English. For example:

“Increase in-house rates 7% because existing residents are currently 12% below street, allowing us to capture dependable revenue while keeping them below market. Increase street rates 3% because occupancy is 95% and the campus remains competitively priced. This narrows the in-house-to-street gap while maximizing the more certain in-house revenue first.”

The overall objective is to maximize dependable revenue growth, maintain resident retention, keep existing residents reasonably close to market rates, and then use street pricing strategically based on occupancy and competitive conditions.
